Featured case study · NHS / University of Leicester

Making clinical data understandable and safer to analyse.

As group lead for a four-student MSc project with University Hospitals of Leicester NHS Trust, I helped design and deliver a Streamlit/Plotly analytics platform. We turned eight years of paediatric emergency activity into a navigable, documented product. It was more than a static slide deck.

The challenge

Analyse 526,805 historical visits alongside 59,459 newer POPS observations, explain differences between clinical warning scores, and protect sensitive data throughout the workflow.

My contribution

I led integration and owned SQ1. This workstream compared POPS with ED NPEWS. Across 50,928 observations scoreable by both systems, I examined transition bands, disagreement patterns and scoring-domain differences.

What we found

Approximately 38% of the POPS 4 to 7 subgroup fell in the NPEWS Low band under our comparison approach. This identifies a scoring-alignment question for clinicians, not proof of patient harm. The wider team also investigated admission models, forecasting and frequent attenders.

What shipped

An interactive analytics experience with 78 charts, a data-quality section, a new-data scoring workflow and a dashboard assistant, backed by testable Python analysis functions and documented chart interpretations.

Four questions. One connected dashboard.

SQ1 / My research workstream
Do POPS and ED NPEWS tell the same story?

Compared scoring bands and domain behaviour, including a 38% Low-band reclassification within the POPS 4 to 7 subgroup.

SQ2 / Team workstream
Who is likely to need admission?

Admission prediction assessed against a POPS-only baseline, with results evaluated on held-out data.

SQ3 / Team workstream
Can emergency demand be anticipated?

SARIMA forecasts were compared with actual February 2026 observations in a held-out period.

SQ4 / Team workstream
Who returns repeatedly, and why?

5,942 children with 10+ attendances made up approximately 16% of visits, motivating subgroup exploration.

Ownership and rigour: My SQ1 analysis and group-lead/integration role are distinct from the team's other workstreams. Score-band comparisons are exploratory; the team did not claim that a dashboard finding alone established clinical harm. Data-quality checks documented missingness instead of automatically filling every blank.

02 / The operating principle

Useful beats impressive.

A model can be clever and still fail the people using it. I care about the entire chain from source data to a decision someone can defend.

01 / Understand

Question the source.

Where did it come from? What changed? Who is missing? My first goal is to understand the quality and limitations of the data.

02 / Build

Make it reproducible.

Clean pipelines, documented assumptions, appropriate baselines, tests and models with known failure cases beat a one-off demonstration.

03 / Deliver

Make it usable.

The final output should give a clinician, analyst or manager a clearer next step, not another dashboard they have to decode.

Trust is part of the architecture.

Working with clinical records taught me to treat pseudonymisation, access controls, validation and re-identification risk as design decisions. They should not be paperwork added at the end.
